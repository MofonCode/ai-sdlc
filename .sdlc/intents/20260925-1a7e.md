# Charter review — the interrogator roles become charter data, and `gaps` learns the artifact

## What is wrong

Two problems, and they have to be fixed together.

**1. The `gaps` role is the vaguest of the four and the only one that should vary.** Three
roles read the same evidence whatever they are pointed at: `charter` reads the charter,
`coherence` reads the artifact alone, `facts` reads the tree. `gaps` reads *"domain reasoning
about what this kind of artifact must cover"* — which is not evidence, it is a gesture at
evidence. What counts as missing differs completely between an intent and a plan, and nothing
says how.

The first interrogation is consistent with this: `gaps` produced 5 findings against
`coherence`'s 10 and `facts`' 10, and 4 of its 5 were capped at `suggested`. A role told to
reason about an unspecified domain produces unfounded-feeling findings, which is exactly its
declared characteristic failure — inventing requirements nobody asked for.

**2. The roles are a second list with nothing binding it.** This document's role table is
prose; `workflows/interrogate.js` hardcodes the same four roles in JavaScript, with a comment
admitting it. They already disagreed once: charter v1's `form` question omitted `Applies-to:`
while the shipped role asked for both clauses, which is finding `r2` of the first
interrogation. That is INV-03, in the document that declares INV-03.

## Why these cannot be separated

Adding per-artifact `gaps` data to this document while the implementation still hardcodes its
roles would **introduce a third representation of the same fact without the check that binds
them** — precisely what INV-03 forbids, and "in the same change" is the load-bearing part of
that clause.

So this intent carries the charter edit *and* the mechanism, in one change:

| Change | Where |
|---|---|
| Per-artifact "what counts as a gap" table | `.sdlc/CHARTER.md`, Interrogator roles |
| Parse the role table out of the charter | `tools/Get-SdlcFacts.ps1` |
| Read roles from the attached facts rather than hardcoding | `workflows/interrogate.js` |
| Fail when the charter's roles and the implementation's disagree | `tests/Sdlc.Tests` |

This is the deferred item the charter already names ("Interrogator roles as charter data"),
and the trigger it recorded has arrived: a retro is not needed to see this, because the drift
was observed directly.

## What is explicitly NOT changing

**The four roles stay orthogonal by question.** They are not becoming personas — no product
owner, architect or developer lens. Considered and rejected, for reasons worth recording so
this does not get re-proposed:

- **Personas overlap and dump deduplication on the merge.** A product owner and an architect
  reading one intent both report "this is vague" as two findings. Two questions cannot do
  that: "does it contradict itself" and "do its claims about the tree hold" have no shared
  output. Measured: the first interrogation produced 41 candidates with **1** exact duplicate.
- **A persona names no evidence and no failure mode.** Every current role specifies both. "Act
  as a product owner" specifies neither, so the agent invents what it means.
- **The taxonomy already encodes the personas.** `missing-case`, `ambiguity` and `untestable`
  *are* the product-owner concerns; `scope-drift` and `charter-violation` are the architect's.
  A persona axis would partly restate the category axis, which is a second list again.

**Interrogation stays a conformance check, not a desirability one.** A product-owner lens
naturally asks "would anyone actually want this?", and that question is reserved for a person
at the intent gate. An interrogator that raises it produces blocking findings nobody can
repair, because the disagreement is about value rather than conformance.

## What the per-artifact table must contain

Not settled here — the wording is the charter edit and belongs to whoever approves this. The
shape is one row per artifact kind, each naming what an absence looks like concretely enough
that a finding can cite it. For example, for an intent: a described behaviour with no failure
mode; success stated adjectivally rather than observably; no stated exclusion.

The kinds themselves are **not** restated: the findings schema's `artifactKind` owns that
list, and this document already says it does not duplicate it.

## A measurement to take while the mechanism is being built

On an intent, `facts` has almost nothing to check — an intent makes few claims about the tree
— and `charter` can only load INV-01 and INV-02, the only invariants whose `Applies-to`
reaches an intent. So four roles may be producing two and a half roles' worth of findings, at
four roles' cost, and the first interrogation cost 2.6M tokens.

Record the per-role yield per artifact kind. If a role reliably produces nothing on a given
kind, dropping it there is a charter change with evidence behind it rather than a guess. Do
not drop anything on the strength of this intent.
